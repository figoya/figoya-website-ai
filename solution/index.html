We structure meaning first. Then language models do what they’re good at.

Our Solution

Current AI asks a language model to construct meaning every time you query it. Figoya removes that step — the meaning-work happens once, at ingestion.

Retrieve meaning, not text. Everything else follows.

Build the graph

Figoya Docs is the lightweight way in

Upload a document — a brief, a contract, a research summary, a memo, a specification — and Figoya returns a structured version. Claims are made explicit, each anchored to the paragraph it came from. Equivalent claims are linked. Contradictions and gaps are surfaced. Ambiguity is flagged rather than hidden.

You get two artefacts: a structured rendering you can read and review, and a machine-readable version optimised for downstream AI consumption — your own language model produces noticeably better answers when fed the structured version than the raw text. Behaviour change required of the user: none. Upload in, structured document out.

Figoya Ingest scales that to an organisation

Documents, emails, chats, connected systems — an organisation’s full corpus structured into a single permission-aware claim graph. The graph is the asset. Every new document strengthens it. Permissions are enforced architecturally, not bolted on as tags.

Use the graph

  1. Figoya Create

    Claim-level collaborative authoring. Multiple authors contribute claims, each with their own lineage. Structural disagreement is surfaced as graph edges, not buried in track-changes. Git for code, Figma for design, Figoya for knowledge.

  2. Figoya People

    Identity, trust and expertise as first-class citizens of the graph. Who contributed what, what they’re credentialed for, who relies on whose claims — all queryable, all structural.

  3. Figoya Market

    Discovery, distribution, licensing and monetisation of structured knowledge. Knowledge becomes an asset that can be found, valued and exchanged.

  4. Figoya Chat

    Team conversation with the graph underneath. Conversations feed the graph; the graph feeds the conversations. Like Slack, except the record holds together.

What falls out of the design

  1. Determinism

    The same question yields the same meaning, every time. The wording the language model produces may vary; the substance underneath does not.

  2. Lineage

    Every claim in every answer is traceable back to its source. You can prove what an answer rests on, claim-by-claim. Audit-grade by construction.

  3. Ceiling

    Better language models make Figoya better at explaining. They don’t change what the answer rests on — that is the architecture’s job. Figoya gains from every language-model advance and owns the layer underneath.
